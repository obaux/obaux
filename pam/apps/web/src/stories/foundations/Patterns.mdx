# Patterns

Rules that came from looking at a real screen. Each says what to do, and where it came from. A pattern with a
component has a story under Components.

## A list of settings (D-615)

Will, 18 October, on Text alerts: *"Remove the card; it isn't needed. Each kind is its own item on the page, separated
by lines."* and *"Move the badge below the row's name, so it fits longer text, especially in other languages."*
Then: *"Please learn from this so the team can improve their design skills."*

**Scope (Will, 18 October, 19:22: "No that's not what I meant. Keep the original layouts.").** These notes were about
Text alerts and a list of settings like it. Other screens keep the layouts they have: a card around a form or a list
elsewhere (Something is wrong here, the reminders list, the program pages) stays as it is unless Will asks for
that screen. Nothing in the card audit changes.

1. **A list of settings has no card.** A card holds a thing of its own: a person, an offer, a place. A list of
   switches on a screen of its own is the page: rows go straight on it, each under a thin line (`SettingsList`,
   `SettingsRow` in `@pam/ui`). This is for lists of settings; it is not a rule for every screen.
2. **A badge goes under the name, not beside it.** Beside it, the badge takes room from the name and a long name in
   Spanish or Russian is cut or squeezed. Under it, the name has the whole width (D-612, `StatusBadge`).
3. **No instructions repeated after first consent.** STOP, HELP and rates are said once, where somebody agrees to
   texts (Sage's rule, in `docs/voice.md` and Foundations › Writing). The agree screen is the only opt-in: a switch on
   a settings screen never records a first yes. With no yes on file it opens the agree screen for the person's own
   role and comes back with that switch on after "Agree", off after "Not now". After a STOP the switches stay off
   and cannot be turned on from the screen.
4. **No legal links on a settings or task screen, with no exception.** Privacy and Terms are in Profile › Legal; a link to them
   where someone is choosing something distracts from the choice (the agree screens carry none either: carriers
   do not require it). Sage's wording of this is in `docs/voice.md` and Foundations › Writing.
5. **Not-yet rows go to the bottom.** What is on comes first; rows with a "Coming soon" badge sit below them, in their
   own order. The list opens on what a person can use, and what is not ready never pushes it down (Will, 18 October).
6. **A label over a list says what the list is**, in one line (D-608), never how Pam works.

The row is laid out in logical directions only, so it mirrors in Arabic. It is checked at 320px with the longest
language, where the name wraps and the row grows.

## When Pam fails (D-617, from Will's D-616 job)

Will, 18 October: *"Use the smaller alert banner. Overlaid below at bottom of page. Let's set this as an alert pattern
for malfunctions."* and *"The switch shouldn't turn on if the connection is not established."*

1. **A malfunction is Pam failing**, a save, a switch or a send. Say it with the small alert, once, with no reason
   and no Contact Pam button (`notice.malfunction`).
2. **It sits at the bottom**, above the tab bar, the safe area and the keyboard, never over the control that failed.
3. **No timer.** It goes on the next tap or key, a successful retry, or leaving the screen.
4. **A switch moves only after its save succeeds**, and shows it is saving. No answer in 15 s is a failure; a late
   success is read back. A heart, star, language picker or role switcher that saves at once follows the same rule.
5. **A reason the person can fix stays by the field**; a page that cannot load keeps its card; a failed message keeps
   "Not sent" on the bubble.
6. **Every failure is logged with a code only**, through `useMalfunction().show`, which is the only way to show it.

Seen in Components › Feedback › Malfunction alert.
