# Patterns

Rules that came from looking at a real screen. Each says what to do, and where it came from. A pattern with a
component has a story under Components.

## A list of settings (D-615)

Will, 18 October, on Text alerts: *"Remove the card; it isn't needed. Each kind is its own item on the page, separated
by lines."* and *"Move the badge below the row's name, so it fits longer text, especially in other languages."*
Then: *"Please learn from this so the team can improve their design skills."*

1. **No card around a single list.** A card holds a thing of its own: a person, an offer, a place. A list of
   settings on a screen of its own is the page. Rows go straight on it, each under a thin line (`SettingsList`,
   `SettingsRow` in `@pam/ui`).
2. **A badge goes under the name, not beside it.** Beside it, the badge takes room from the name and a long name in
   Spanish or Russian is cut or squeezed. Under it, the name has the whole width (D-612, `StatusBadge`).
3. **No instructions repeated after first consent.** STOP, HELP and rates are said where somebody agrees to texts
   (and a carrier needs them there). A settings screen that comes after does not say them again. If the screen can
   be reached without the agreement, the first switch-on goes to the agreement; it does not carry the lines itself.
4. **No legal links on a task screen.** Privacy and Terms are in Profile › Legal. A link to them on a screen where
   someone is choosing something distracts from the choice.
5. **A label over a list says what the list is**, in one line (D-608), never how Pam works.

The row is laid out in logical directions only, so it mirrors in Arabic. It is checked at 320px with the longest
language, where the name wraps and the row grows.
