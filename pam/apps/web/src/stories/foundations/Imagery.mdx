# Imagery

Every picture Pam ships, in one place, for handoff: the illustrations, the photographs and
commissioned art, the brand marks and the example people. Each one can be downloaded from here:
a file as it ships, or a vector illustration as an SVG with its colours written in.

**It can't go out of date.** The illustration sets are drawn from the code's own lists
(`SETUP_ART_KINDS`, `CATEGORIES`, `BADGE_ART_KEYS`), and the files from `imagery.ts`, which a
test keeps in step with `apps/web/public`. Before you draw or add a picture, check it isn't
already here.

## Illustrations — the language

- **Flat colour, lit from the left.** Each object is two tones: the lit side and the shade
  (D-297). No glow, no gradients.
- **One object per picture, on a ground.** A flat ground and up to two diagonal shards behind it.
- **A print grain** over every picture (D-298, D-299): 30% at card size.
- **Shapes, not strokes.** Arrows and marks are solid, chunky, two-tone shapes, not lines
  (D-376).
- **One kit.** Every vector picture is drawn with `art/kit.tsx`: its colours (`--color-data-*`,
  which have light and dark pairs), its grain and its frame. Don't hand-roll an SVG.
- **Decorative.** Pictures are `aria-hidden`. The words next to them carry the meaning.

## In a banner — the hero template

`SetupArt isHero` inside `SubPage`'s `hero` (240px, full width). The ground fills the banner,
the subject is drawn smaller at the centre, and **the grain is quieter (10%)**, because at
banner size the full grain reads as dirt. This holds for every illustration used in a large
banner (D-376).

## Adding one

**A file** (photo, art, mark): put it in `apps/web/public`, add it to `imagery.ts` with where it
is used and its decision — the inventory test fails until you do.

**An illustration:**

1. Draw it with `art/kit.tsx` in the language above. Keep the subject inside the middle band
   so a banner crop never cuts it.
2. Add it to its set (`SETUP_ART_KINDS`, a category, or `BadgeArt`'s map) — it then shows up
   on this page by itself.
3. Give it a line in its set's notes here (where it's used, and the D-number).
4. Check it at card size and, if it will be used in a banner, with `isHero`.
